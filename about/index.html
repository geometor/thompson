Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

The GEOMETOR edition of D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson and Jay Hambidge establishes a rigorous, unabridged, folio-verified Digital Scriptorium critical edition of On Growth and Form (Cambridge University Press, First Edition, 1917) paired with The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry (Yale University Press, 1919–1920) as the biological and morphological keystone of the GEOMETOR ecosystem. D’Arcy Thompson’s monumental treatise stands as the premier synthesis of classical geometry, physical mechanics, and comparative biology: demonstrating how physical forces and geometric laws govern organic architecture—from Galileo’s Square-Cube Law of magnitude to Plateau’s minimal surfaces of equilibrium ($120^\circ$ liquid films and $109^\circ 28' 16''$ polyhedral junctions), the honeybee’s rhombic dodecahedral cell, Lord Kelvin’s minimal-foam tetrakaidecahedron, Haeckel’s siliceous Radiolaria skeletons, Bernoulli’s equiangular spiral ($r = a e^{\theta \cot\alpha}$), Hero of Alexandria’s gnomons, Fibonacci phyllotaxis, Culmann crane stress trajectories in mammalian femur trabeculae, and Cartesian coordinate transformation grids mapping morphogenetic transitions between related biological species.

Paired alongside Thompson’s morphological masterpiece is Jay Hambidge’s rediscovery of dynamic symmetry—the proportional system underlying classical Greek art, architecture, and natural phyllotaxis based on root rectangles ($\sqrt{1}\dots\sqrt{5}$) and golden ratio ($\phi$) whirling squares. Hambidge demonstrates that ancient design was not governed by static, arithmetical moduler units, but by dynamic geometric relationships derived directly from nature’s growth laws.

This digital critical edition extracts the implicit structure of both treatises into an addressable semantic network. All 17 chapters, 408 figures, authorial endnotes, and 11 calibrated SVG plates are encoded with stable identifiers and inline folio milestones (pp. 1–779). This edition connects biological morphogenesis directly to the broader GEOMETOR corpus: classical Euclidean proportion in Euclid's Elements, golden sections and gnomonic growth in geometor/divine, Fermat–Torricelli minimal foam junctions $X(13)$ in geometor/etc, exact rational spreads and root-rectangle quadrances in geometor/wildberger, botanical parastichy lattices in geometor/phyllotaxis, and projective transformation groups in geometor/pappus and geometor.staudt.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

GEOMETOR is an ongoing research initiative founded by phi ARCHITECT dedicated to recovering, formalizing, and revitalizing the world’s mathematical and geometric heritage through three foundational pillars:

  1. Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge: Moving beyond passive page scans and OCR to recover the living deductive structure of 2,300+ years of geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises—elevating prose, marginal citations, primary-source folios (via publish folio), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector .euclid-graphic diagrams into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs.

  2. Exact Symbolic Grounding (``geometor.model``): Anchoring geometric constructions in 100% exact symbolic algebra (SymPy) and Rational Trigonometry invariants (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio) over exact algebraic fields ($\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$) rather than floating-point approximations. In morphology, this symbolic rigor models dynamic symmetry root rectangles and minimal foam equilibrium angles with algebraic precision.

  3. Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums: Re-deploying extracted semantic and algebraic structures across synchronized multi-track Book Codices and EPUB 3 editions (photon-platform/publish), computable deductive canons (geometor.elements, geometor.etc, geometor.pappus, geometor.divine), interactive SVG laboratories (geometor.explorer), QHD visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis (geometor.seer) across geometor.com.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology¶

This Digital Scriptorium edition is anchored strictly to 100% Public Domain first printings verified via SHA-256 checksums in resources/growth_and_form/sources_manifest.json and 300 DPI multimodal scan inspection:

  1. D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson, *On Growth and Form* (Cambridge: at the University Press, 1917) — First Edition, xv + 793 pages, 17 chapters, 408 figures (LCCN 18001383; Internet Archive ongrowthform1917thom; Project Gutenberg eBook #55264).

  2. Jay Hambidge, *The Diagonal* (Vol. I, Nos. 1–12, New Haven: Yale University Press, Nov. 1919 – Oct. 1920) — Original serial publication of The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry (Getty Research Institute digitization diagonal1191unse).

  3. Jay Hambidge, *Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase* (New Haven: Yale University Press, 1920) — First book monograph on static vs. dynamic symmetry, sunflower phyllotaxis, root rectangles, and whirling squares (University of Toronto digitization dynamicsymmetry00hamb).
