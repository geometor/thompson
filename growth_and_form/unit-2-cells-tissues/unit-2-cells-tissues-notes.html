Unit II: Capillarity, Cellular Foams, the Bee’s Cell, and Spicular Skeletons — Notes & Concordance¶

Critical Apparatus & GEOMETOR Cross-References — II. Cells, Foams & Honeycomb

Scholarly Commentary & GEOMETOR Concordance (Unit II)¶

Rational Trigonometry of Soap Films (geometor/etc & geometor/wildberger)¶

  • 2D Triple Junctions (`X(13)` & Spread $s = 3/4$): Three co-planar soap films spanning vertices $A, B, C$ meet at the first Fermat–Torricelli–Steiner point X(13) catalogued in geometor/etc. In Wildberger's Rational Trigonometry (geometor/wildberger), the $120^\circ$ equilibrium angle requires zero transcendental functions: its exact algebraic spread is $s = \sin^2(120^\circ) = 3/4 \in \mathbb{Q}$.

  • 3D Quadruple Junctions & the Maraldi Angle (Spread $s = 8/9$): Four liquid edges meeting at a spatial foam vertex—or the three rhombi meeting at the trihedral apex of the bee’s cell (verified on 300 DPI scan p. 330, Fig. 132)—subtend $\theta = \arccos(-1/3) \approx 109^\circ 28' 16.39''$. Since $\cos^2\theta = 1/9$, the tetrahedral Maraldi angle has exact rational spread:

    $$s = 1 - \cos^2\theta = \frac{8}{9}$$

    Each face of Kepler's rhombic dodecahedron has short-to-long diagonal ratio $1 : \sqrt{2}$ (diagonal quadrance ratio $1 : 2$), linking Unit II directly to Jay Hambidge's root-two rectangle in Unit VII!

Euler’s Polyhedral Law & Haeckel’s Aulonia hexagona (geometor/euclid & geometor/pacioli)¶

  • Impossible Pure-Hexagon Spheres (Ch. IX, p. 473): One of Thompson’s sharpest mathematical proofs refutes Haeckel's claim that the radiolarian Aulonia hexagona builds a closed sphere of uniform hexagons. For any 3-valent convex polyhedral mesh ($3V = 2E$) satisfying Euler’s formula $V - E + F = 2$, summing face degrees $F = \sum F_k$ and $2E = \sum k F_k$ gives the topological invariant:

    $$\sum_{k \ge 3} (6 - k) F_k = 12$$

    Because $(6 - 6) = 0$, hexagons contribute nothing to the curvature defect $12$: every closed hexagonal cage (whether radiolarian silica, viral capsid, geodesic dome, or $\mathrm{C}_{60}$ fullerene) must incorporate pentagons (e.g., exactly $12$ pentagons when $k \in \{5, 6\}$), bridging directly to Book XIII of geometor/euclid and Leonardo’s polyhedra in geometor/pacioli.

Structured Concordance Loci in This Unit¶

Capillarity, Laplace–Young Law, and Plateau–Delaunay Surfaces of Revolution (Chs. V–VI (Forms of Cells & Adsorption), pp. 201–292)

Classifies solitary unicellular outlines—Amoeba, Lagena, Ciliates, Flagellates—through the six axisymmetric surfaces of constant mean curvature. Algebraic Invariants: ΔP = T (1/R₁ + 1/R₂) · H = const (sphere, cylinder, catenoid, unduloid, nodoid)

  • geometor/pappus (Synagoge Book V (Isoperimetric Figures in Plane and Space)): Extends classical Alexandrian isoperimetric minimization (circle and sphere) to liquid membranes under pressure and tension.

Plateau’s 120° Triple Junction, Steiner Networks, and Hexagonal Epithelia (Chs. VII–VIII (Forms of Tissues, or Cell-Aggregates), pp. 293–326, 346–410)

Shows that hexagonal cellular pavements, segmentation cleavage planes (Sachs’s & Errera’s laws), and epithelial mosaics arise directly from liquid surface-tension equilibrium. Algebraic Invariants: ∠APB = ∠BPC = ∠CPA = 120° · s(120°) = 3/4 · Euler V - E + F = χ

  • geometor/etc (X(13) (1st Isogonic / Fermat–Torricelli–Steiner Point)): Three soap-film laminae spanning three pins converge physically at X(13), subtending 120° between every pair of segments.

  • geometor/wildberger (Rational Spread s = 3/4): The 120° soap-film equilibrium angle corresponds to exact rational spread sin²(120°) = 3/4 over Q.

  • geometor/pappus (Synagoge Book V, Preface (On the Sagacity of Bees)): Pappus’s proof that the regular hexagon encloses maximum area per unit perimeter among plane-tiling polygons becomes physical capillary minimization in Thompson.

  • Dynamic Symmetry Parallel: Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase (1920), Ch. I — hexagonal crystal/honeycomb tiling as the archetype of √3 Static Symmetry

Maraldi’s Tetrahedral Angle (109° 28′ 16″), Rhombic Dodecahedron, and Kelvin’s Tetrakaidecahedron (Ch. VII (The Bee’s Cell & Parenchyma), pp. 327–345)

Unifies Kepler, Maraldi, Réaumur, König, Maclaurin, Boscovich, Plateau, and Kelvin: both the bee’s pyramidal cell cap and three-dimensional soap froth obey the tetrahedral spread s = 8/9. Algebraic Invariants: cos θ = -1/3 · tan(θ/2) = √2 · s = 8/9 · 14-hedron: F = 14, V = 24, E = 36

  • geometor/wildberger (Tetrahedral Rational Spread s = 8/9): The Maraldi angle arccos(-1/3) ≈ 109° 28′ 16″ has cos²θ = 1/9 and exact rational spread s = 1 - 1/9 = 8/9, with rhombus diagonal quadrance ratio 1 : 2.

  • geometor/pacioli (De Divina Proportione (1509) & Kepler Strena (1611)): Connects semi-regular/space-filling polyhedra—Kepler’s rhombic dodecahedron and Kelvin’s truncated octahedron (8 hexagons + 6 squares)—to physical foam packing.

  • Dynamic Symmetry Parallel: The Diagonal (1919–1920), p. 18 — 1 : √2 diagonal ratio of the Maraldi rhombus matches the root-two rectangle

Sponge Spicules, Haeckel’s Polyhedral Radiolaria, Euler’s Formula, and Cell Geodetics (Chs. IX–X (Spicules, Radiolaria & Geodetics), pp. 411–492)

Proves via Euler’s theorem that no spherical radiolarian mesh can consist solely of hexagons, and explains tetrahedral/octahedral/icosahedral baskets via symmetrical泡-film partitions. Algebraic Invariants: V - E + F = 2 · ∑ (6 - k) F_k = 12 (3-valent spherical net)

  • geometor/euclid (Book XIII, Props. 13–18 (Construction & Exhaustion of the Five Regular Solids)): Critiques Haeckel’s claim of true hexagonal/octagonal regular lattices on spheres via Euler’s polyhedral theorem (12 pentagons mandatory in hexagonal fullerene/radiolarian cages, e.g. Aulonia hexagona).

  • geometor/pacioli (De Divina Proportione, Skeletal Polyhedra Plates): Callimitra and Challenger radiolarian frames physically instantiate skeletal wire-frame polyhedra via adsorbed siliceous deposition along Plateau film edges.

  • Dynamic Symmetry Parallel: Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase (1920) — icosahedral/dodecahedral √5 proportions in nature
