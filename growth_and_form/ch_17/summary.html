Chapter XVII: On the Theory of Transformations, or the Comparison of Related Forms — Summary¶

Analytical Synopsis — 1917 Cambridge First Edition — Chapter XVII & Epilogue (pp. 719–779)

Overview of Chapter XVII & Epilogue (The Theory of Transformations)¶

Chapter XVII and the Epilogue present the climax of On Growth and Form—the Method of Coordinates and the philosophy of mathematical morphology:

  1. The Method of Coordinates (pp. 719–735): Elevates Albrecht Dürer's proportion grids (durer:1528) to dynamic transformations $(x', y') = (f(x, y), g(x, y))$, classifying affine shears, radial expansions, and coaxial transformations.

  2. Comparative Phylogeny (pp. 736–777): Demonstrates global coordinate deformations across crustacean carapaces, teleost fishes (Argyropelecus to Sternoptyx, Diodon to Orthagoriscus), and reptile/mammalian skulls (thompson:1915).

  3. Epilogue (pp. 778–779): Thompson’s lyric meditation linking Pythagorean philosophy, Milton’s golden compasses, Sylvester, and Jean-Henri Fabre (Maclaurin).
