Chapter V: The Forms of Cells — Summary¶

Analytical Synopsis — 1917 Cambridge First Edition — Chapter V (pp. 201–276)

Overview of Chapter V (The Forms of Cells)¶

Chapter V applies capillary surface tension and minimal-surface geometry to solitary unicellular organisms:

  1. The Laplace–Young Law (pp. 201–220): Formulates the fundamental pressure equation $\Delta P = T(1/R_1 + 1/R_2)$ governing fluid membranes under surface tension $T$.

  2. Plateau–Delaunay Surfaces of Revolution (pp. 221–250): Classifies the six axisymmetric surfaces of constant mean curvature—plane, sphere, cylinder, catenoid, unduloid, and nodoid (plateau:1873).

  3. Unicellular Morphology (pp. 251–276): Interprets the outlines of solitary protozoa (Amoeba, Lagena, ciliates, flagellates, and mammalian erythrocytes) as capillary equilibrium surfaces.
