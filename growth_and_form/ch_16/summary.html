Chapter XVI: On Form and Mechanical Efficiency — Summary¶

Analytical Synopsis — 1917 Cambridge First Edition — Chapter XVI (pp. 670–718)

Overview of Chapter XVI (On Form and Mechanical Efficiency)¶

Chapter XVI explores biomechanical structural optimization, graphic statics, and skeletal architecture:

  1. Culmann’s Crane & Meyer’s Femur (pp. 670–693): Documents the historic connection between engineer Karl Culmann (culmann:1866) and anatomist Hermann von Meyer (meyer:1867), demonstrating that human femur head trabeculae align along orthogonal principal stress trajectories.

  2. Vertebrate Backbones as Cantilevers (pp. 694–712): Collaborating with Dundee engineer T. Claxton Fidler (fidler:1887), models quadruped and dinosaur skeletons as two-armed cantilever bridges.

  3. Streamlined Aquatic Forms (pp. 713–718): Analyzes hydrodynamic streamlines in fish and cetaceans.
