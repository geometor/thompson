Unit V: Hollow Structures, Culmann’s Crane, and Mechanical Efficiency — Summary¶

Analytical Synopsis — 1917 Cambridge First Edition — Chapters XV–XVI (pp. 652–718, Complete Verbatim Text)

Overview of Unit V (Chapters XV–XVI — Complete Verbatim Text)¶

Unit V reproduces verbatim Chapter XV (*On the Shapes of Eggs, and of Certain Other Hollow Structures*, pp. 652–669) and Chapter XVI (*On Form and Mechanical Efficiency*, pp. 670–718):

  1. Chapter XV — On the Shapes of Eggs and Hollow Structures (pp. 652–669):

    • Refutes teleological myths that pyriform guillemot eggs evolved solely to spin in circles on cliff ledges; instead derives spherical, elliptical, oval, and conical bird-egg profiles from the Laplace membrane equilibrium equation:

      $$\frac{T_n}{R_n} + \frac{T_t}{R_t} = P_n$$

      under peristaltic muscular waves pushing the still-pliable shell membrane blunt-end-forward down the oviduct.

    • Extends axisymmetric elastic-shell mechanics to sea-urchin tests (Echinus, flattened fluid drop under gravity) and hydra/medusa bells.

  2. Chapter XVI — On Form and Mechanical Efficiency (pp. 670–718):

    • Culmann’s Crane & Meyer’s Femur (pp. 679–688, Fig. 335): Chronicles the historic 1866 Zürich meeting between anatomist Hermann von Meyer (meyer:1867) and engineer Karl Culmann (culmann:1866), showing that the spongy trabeculae of the human femur head align along the exact orthogonal principal stress trajectories of a curved Fairbairn crane—eliminating shearing stress along bony struts and dynamically remodeling when fractures alter load lines (Wolff’s Law).

    • Vertebrate Backbones as Balanced Cantilever Bridges (pp. 694–718): Working directly with Dundee bridge engineer T. Claxton Fidler (fidler:1887), Thompson models quadruped, stegosaur, Diplodocus, and avian skeletons as two-armed cantilevers and Forth-Bridge reciprocal trusses where vertebral centra take compression and supraspinous ligaments take tension, concluding with streamlined fish contours (Parsons & Haushofer).
