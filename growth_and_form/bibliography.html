Bibliography¶

Primary 1917 / 1920 Editions, Classical Treatises & Morphological Monographs

St. Thomas Aquinas — Quaestiones Disputatae de Potentia Dei

Quaestio III, Articulus 11 (‘Cum formarum naturalium et corporalium esse non consistat nisi in unione ad materiam…’), Rome / Paris (quoted in Thompson 1917 Epigraph), c. 1265.

Primary Scholastic epigraph opening the 1917 Cambridge edition, grounding Thompson’s thesis that natural physical causes suffice to generate corporeal forms.

Archimedes of Syracuse — On Spirals (De Lineis Spiralibus)

Definitions & Propositions 1–28, Critical ed. J. L. Heiberg (Teubner); English ed. T. L. Heath (Cambridge, 1897), c. 225 BC.

Defines the uniform arithmetic spiral r = aθ, contrasted by Thompson (Ch. XI, pp. 494–498) with the geometric growth law of the equiangular spiral.

Aristotle — Categoriae (Categories)

Ch. 14, 15a29–33, Corpus Aristotelicum (Bekker pagination), c. 350 BC.

States that a square, when a gnomon is placed around it, is increased in size (auxanetai) without being altered in form (alloioutai)—the philosophical kernel of Chapter XI.

Aristotle — Historia Animalium (History of Animals)

Translated by D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson (The Works of Aristotle, Vol. IV), Clarendon Press, Oxford, 1910 (orig. c. 343 BC).

Thompson’s own landmark English translation of Aristotle’s zoology, informing observations on shells, horns, cetaceans, and comparative anatomy across On Growth and Form.

Jakob Bernoulli — Lineae Cycloidales, Evolutae, Ant-Evolutae, Causticae, Anti-Causticae, Peri-Causticae (Spira Mirabilis)

Acta Eruditorum, May 1692, pp. 207–213, Leipzig, 1692.

Demonstrates the invariance of the logarithmic spiral under geometric operations and concludes with the motto Eadem mutata resurgo (Ch. XI, p. 504).

Roger Joseph Boscovich — Supplement on the Cells of Bees (in Benedict Stay, Philosophiae recentioris versibus traditae libri X, Tom. II)

Annot. in Lib. V, Pagliarini, Rome, 1760.

Examines Maraldi’s honeycomb angle and shows the mathematical sensitivity of the wax minimum around 109° 28′ 16″ (Ch. VII, pp. 332–335).

Louis Bravais & Auguste Bravais — Essai sur la disposition des feuilles curvisériées

Annales des Sciences Naturelles (Botanique), 2e série, Tome 7, pp. 42–110, Crochard, Paris, 1837.

Establishes cylindrical lattice geometry and the unique irrational golden divergence angle in spiral phyllotaxis (Ch. XIV).

Arthur Harry Church — On the Relation of Phyllotaxis to Mechanical Laws

Parts I–III (pp. 1–353), Williams & Norgate, London, 1904.

Models apical phyllotaxis via orthogonal families of logarithmic spirals; cited both by Thompson (Ch. XIV, pp. 646–650) and by Jay Hambidge (The Diagonal, Vol. I, No. 1, 1919).

Karl Culmann — Die graphische Statik

1st ed., Zürich, Meyer & Zeller, Zürich, 1866.

Foundational treatise of graphic statics whose Fairbairn crane stress trajectories match the cancellous trabeculae of the human femur (Ch. XVI, pp. 679–686).

Albrecht Dürer — Hierinn sind begriffen vier Bücher von menschlicher Proportion

Books III–IV, Hieronymus Andreae (Formschneider), Nuremberg, 1528.

Introduces rectilinear, oblique, and circular coordinate deformations of human heads and bodies, honoured by Thompson (Ch. XVII, pp. 727, 768) as the precursor of transformation grids.

Euclid of Alexandria — Elements (Stoicheia)

Books II (Def. 2, Prop. 11), IV (Prop. 10), VI (Def. 1 & 3, Prop. 30), X (Def. 1–4), XI, XIII, Critical ed. J. L. Heiberg (Teubner, 1883–1888), c. 300 BC.

Classical geometric foundation for gnomons, extreme and mean ratio, the 36°–72°–72° golden triangle, similitude, commensurability in square (dynamis), and the five regular polyhedra.

Thomas Claxton Fidler — A Practical Treatise on Bridge-Construction: Being a Text-Book on the Design and Construction of Bridges in Iron and Steel

Cantilever & Girder Stress Diagrams, Charles Griffin & Co., London, 1887.

Source of the cantilever and Forth-Bridge reciprocal stress frameworks applied by Thompson to vertebrate skeletons and dinosaur spines in Chapter XVI (pp. 694–712).

Galileo Galilei — Discorsi e Dimostrazioni Matematiche, intorno à due nuove scienze

Giornata Seconda (Strength of Beams & Scaling of Bones), Elsevirii, Leiden, 1638.

Formulates the Square-Cube Law governing maximum size of trees, terrestrial quadrupeds, and marine cetaceans (Ch. II, pp. 19–28).

Ernst Haeckel — Report on the Radiolaria Collected by H.M.S. Challenger during the Years 1873–1876

Zoology, Vol. XVIII (2 parts + 140 plates), H.M. Stationery Office, London, 1887.

Monumental Challenger monograph whose polyhedral and spicular Radiolaria are analyzed physically via liquid-film geometry in Chapter IX (pp. 468–487).

Jay Hambidge — The Diagonal: An Illustrated Monthly Magazine Devoted to the Explanation of the Rediscovered Principles of Greek Design (‘The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry’)

Vol. I, Nos. 1–12, Yale University Press, New Haven, 1919–1920.

Primary public-domain serial source of Hambidge’s Elements of Dynamic Symmetry lessons: sunflower phyllotaxis, root-rectangles (√1–√5), reciprocal diagonals, and whirling squares.

Jay Hambidge — Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase

Chs. I–III (Static vs. Dynamic Symmetry, Phyllotaxis, Root-Five & Whirling-Square Rectangles), Yale University Press, New Haven & New York, 1920.

Connects biological growth laws (shells and phyllotaxis) directly to Greek commensurability in square (Euclid Book X) and classical harmonic design.

Sir Thomas Little Heath — The Thirteen Books of Euclid’s Elements, Translated from the Text of Heiberg with Introduction and Commentary

Vol. I (Book II Def. 2, pp. 370–372); Vol. II (Book IV.10, VI.30); Vol. III (Books X & XIII), Cambridge University Press, 1908.

Standard Cambridge philological commentary cited alongside Thompson’s historical reconstruction of Greek gnomonic geometry.

Hero of Alexandria — Definitiones (Heronis Alexandrini Opera quae supersunt omnia, Vol. IV, ed. J. L. Heiberg)

Def. 58, B. G. Teubner, Leipzig, c. AD 60 (ed. 1912).

Preserves the generalized Greek definition of a gnomon as any increment leaving a figure geometrically similar to itself (Ch. XI, p. 509).

Wilhelm Hofmeister — Allgemeine Morphologie der Gewächse

Handbuch der physiologischen Botanik, Bd. I, Abt. 2, Wilhelm Engelmann, Leipzig, 1868.

Formulates Hofmeister’s mechanical gap-rule for leaf primordia at the growing apex (Ch. XIV, pp. 648–651).

Sir William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) — On the Division of Space with Minimum Partitional Area

Philosophical Magazine, 5th series, Vol. 24, No. 151, pp. 503–514, Taylor & Francis, London, 1887.

Demonstrates the minimal-foam space partition by 14-hedra (tetrakaidecahedra: 8 hexagons and 6 squares), applied to cellular parenchyma in Chapters VII–VIII (pp. 338–345).

Johannes Kepler — Strena seu De Nive Sexangula (The Six-Cornered Snowflake)

On Bee Cells, Pomegranate Packing & the Rhombic Dodecahedron, Godfrey Tampach, Frankfurt am Main, 1611.

First geometric description of the space-filling rhombic dodecahedron formed by close-packed spheres and opposed honeycomb layers (Ch. VII, pp. 328–336).

Colin Maclaurin — Of the Bases of the Cells wherein the Bees deposite their Honey

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society, Vol. 42, No. 471, pp. 565–571, Royal Society, London, 1743.

Proves purely by Euclidean geometry that the minimum-surface rhombic cap has obtuse angle 2 arctan(√2) = 109° 28′ 16″ (Ch. VII, p. 332).

Giacomo Filippo Maraldi — Observations sur les Abeilles

Histoire et Mémoires de l’Académie Royale des Sciences (année 1712), pp. 299–335, Imprimerie Royale, Paris, 1712.

Empirical measurement of the rhombic inclination and obtuse dihedral/planar angles (~109° 28′ and 70° 32′) of the honeybee’s cell (Ch. VII, pp. 329–334).

Georg Hermann von Meyer — Die Architektur der Spongiosa

Archiv für Anatomie, Physiologie und wissenschaftliche Medicin (Reicherts und Du Bois-Reymonds Archiv), pp. 615–628, Veit & Comp., Leipzig, 1867.

Reveals the regular curved trajectories of cancellous bone trabeculae in the human femur and calcaneus, paired with Culmann’s crane diagram (Ch. XVI, pp. 679–686).

Henry Moseley — On the Geometrical Forms of Turbinated and Discoid Shells

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London, Vol. 128, pp. 351–370, Royal Society, London, 1838.

Classic analytical study of logarithmic spiral shells and opercula upon which Chapter XI of On Growth and Form builds directly.

Fra Luca Pacioli — De Divina Proportione (illustrations by Leonardo da Vinci)

Pars Prima (Divine Proportion φ & Platonic Polyhedra), Paganino Paganini, Venice, 1509.

Renaissance treatise on extreme and mean ratio and regular polyhedra, bridged in GEOMETOR to Thompson’s gnomons and radiolarian polyhedra.

Pappus of Alexandria — Synagoge (Mathematical Collection, ed. F. Hultsch)

Book V Preface (On the Sagacity of Bees & Isoperimetry); Book VII (Domain of Analysis & Cross-Ratio), Weidmann, Berlin, c. AD 320 (ed. 1876–1878).

Unites the classical isoperimetric proof of the hexagonal honeycomb (Ch. VII, p. 327) with the projective cross-ratio transformations underlying Chapter XVII.

Joseph Antoine Ferdinand Plateau — Statique expérimentale et théorique des liquides soumis aux seules forces moléculaires

Tomes I–II, F. Clemm, Ghent & Gauthier-Villars, Paris, 1873.

Definitive treatise on soap films, minimal surfaces, Delaunay surfaces of constant mean curvature, and cellular foam equilibria (Chs. V–IX).

Peter Guthrie Tait — On Phyllotaxis

Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh, Vol. 7, pp. 391–394, Royal Society of Edinburgh, 1872.

Demonstrates geometrically how overlapping cylindrical parastichy lattices generate Fibonacci recurrence numbers (Ch. XIV, pp. 643–645, Fig. 325).

D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson — Magnalia Naturae; or, The Greater Problems of Biology

Presidential Address to Section D (Zoology), British Association for the Advancement of Science, Portsmouth (also Science, Vol. 34, pp. 417–428), British Association, London, 1911.

Programmatic address introducing the Aquinas epigraph and calling for physical and mathematical morphology prior to On Growth and Form.

D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson — Morphology and Mathematics

Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh, Vol. 50, Part IV, No. 27, pp. 857–895, Robert Grant & Son, Edinburgh, 1915.

First publication of Thompson’s coordinate transformation grids (Argyropelecus/Sternoptyx, Diodon/Orthagoriscus, crab carapaces, Eohippus/Equus), expanded into Chapter XVII of the 1917 edition.

D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson — On Growth and Form

First Edition, xv + 793 pp., 408 figures, Cambridge University Press, Cambridge, 1917.

Authoritative 1917 First Edition reproduced in this Digital Scriptorium Book Codex.

Alan Mathison Turing — The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London, Series B, Vol. 237, No. 641, pp. 37–72, Royal Society, London, 1952.

Foundational reaction-diffusion paper explicitly citing Thompson’s On Growth and Form as inspiration for mathematical morphogenesis and phyllotaxis.
