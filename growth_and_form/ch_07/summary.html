Chapter VII: The Forms of Tissues, or Cell-Aggregates — Summary¶

Analytical Synopsis — 1917 Cambridge First Edition — Chapter VII (pp. 293–345)

Overview of Chapter VII (The Forms of Tissues, or Cell-Aggregates)¶

Chapter VII establishes the geometry of cellular froths, minimal partition surfaces, and the bee’s honeycomb:

  1. Plateau’s Laws for Liquid Films (pp. 293–326): In planar networks, three partitions meet at $120^\circ$ (Fermat–Torricelli point X(13)), creating hexagonal arrangements. In 3D space, four edges meet at tetrahedral angles $\arccos(-1/3) \approx 109^\circ 28' 16''$.

  2. The Bee’s Cell & Honeycomb (pp. 327–336): Traces the mathematical history from Pappus and Kepler (kepler:1611) through Maraldi (maraldi:1712), Maclaurin (maclaurin:1743), and Boscovich (boscovich:1760), proving that the rhombic dodecahedron cap with Maraldi angle minimizes wax area through physical soap-film tension.

  3. Parenchyma & Kelvin’s Foam (pp. 336–345): Examines Plateau’s wire-cube soap film (Fig. 134) and Lord Kelvin's space-filling 14-hedral tetrakaidecahedron (kelvin:1887).
