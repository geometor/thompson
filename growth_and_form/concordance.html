Thompson–Hambidge–GEOMETOR Master Concordance¶

Sir D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson & GEOMETOR Master Concordance

Structured cross-repository concordance mapping Sir D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson’s On Growth and Form (Cambridge University Press, 1st ed., 1917) across the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium and symbolic geometry ecosystem.

Editions Aligned & Copyright Boundary¶

  • thompson_1917_cup: On Growth and Form, First Edition (Cambridge: University Press, 1917, xv + 793 pp., 17 chapters)

Note

Strictly anchored to the 1917 1st Edition (17 chapters); Thompson’s 1942 2nd Edition and Bonner’s 1961 abridgement are strictly excluded.

Cross-Repository Concordance Entries¶

Mathematical Morphology, Efficient Cause, and Physical Law¶

Chapter & Locus: Prefatory Note & Ch. I (pp. v–vi, 1–15)
Summary: Establishes morphology as a mathematical science of physical forces acting in the present and immediate past of the growing organism.

  • Exact Invariants: F = m a, Dimensional homogeneity

  • geometor/aquinas (De Potentia Dei, Q. 3, a. 11): Thompson’s opening Latin epigraph affirms that corporeal forms require no extrinsic agent beyond the physical causes transmuting matter.

  • geometor/aristotle (Physics II & Historia Animalium): Balances Aristotelian formal/teleological morphology with Newtonian and Galilean efficient mechanics.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase (1920), Preface & Introduction — recovering mathematical law behind organic and classical artistic form

Principle of Similitude, Galileo’s Square-Cube Law, and Scale Regimes¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. II (On Magnitude) (pp. 16–49)
Summary: Demonstrates why gravity governs terrestrial vertebrates (L³), surface tension and viscosity govern protists and cells (L² and L¹), and Brownian agitation rules bacteria.

  • Exact Invariants: S / S₀ = (L / L₀)², V / V₀ = (L / L₀)³, S / V ∝ L⁻¹

  • geometor/euclid (Book VI, Def. 1 & Props. 19–20; Book XI, Prop. 33): Duplicate ratio of areas (L²) and triplicate ratio of similar solids (L³) supply the exact geometric basis for Galileo’s scaling bounds.

  • geometor/elements (Book VI Similitude DAG): Formalizes proportional scaling of similar rectilinear and solid figures.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): The Diagonal (1919–1920), Lesson I — invariance of proportional shape versus absolute linear scale

Growth Velocity Curves, Sigmoid Trajectories, and Differential Growth Gradients¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. III (The Rate of Growth) (pp. 50–155)
Summary: Analyzes growth velocity curves, Quetelet’s stature data, and unequal directional growth rates as the ontogenetic driver of morphological deformation.

  • Exact Invariants: dL/dt, dx/dt ≠ dy/dt, Sigmoid autocatalytic curve

  • geometor/euclid (Book V (Theory of Abstract Proportions)): Eudoxian proportional ratios govern differential growth velocities dx/dt and dy/dt across distinct anatomical dimensions.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): The Diagonal (1919–1920) — proportional growth ratios and dynamic progression curves

Mitotic Spindle, Centrosomes, Karyokinetic Asters, and Colloidal Force Lines¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. IV (Internal Form and Structure of the Cell) (pp. 156–200)
Summary: Models mitotic asters and spindle fibers as physical tension lines and dynamic potential fields within protoplasmic colloids.

  • Exact Invariants: Dynamic lines of force, Colloidal sol-gel tension

  • geometor/aristotle (De Anima & Generation of Animals): Physical tension fields in protoplasm replace speculative entelechies in accounting for cellular division configurations.

Capillarity, Laplace–Young Law, and Plateau–Delaunay Surfaces of Revolution¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. V (The Forms of Cells) (pp. 201–276)
Summary: Classifies solitary unicellular outlines—Amoeba, Lagena, Ciliates, Flagellates—through the six axisymmetric surfaces of constant mean curvature.

  • Exact Invariants: ΔP = T (1/R₁ + 1/R₂), H = const (sphere, cylinder, catenoid, unduloid, nodoid)

  • geometor/pappus (Synagoge Book V (Isoperimetric Figures in Plane and Space)): Extends classical Alexandrian isoperimetric minimization (circle and sphere) to liquid membranes under pressure and tension.

Gibbs–Thomson Interfacial Concentration and Cell Wall Deposition¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. VI (A Note on Adsorption) (pp. 277–292)
Summary: Applies Gibbs–Thomson surface thermodynamics to explain how cell membranes, pellicles, and localized mineralizations form at interfaces.

  • Exact Invariants: Gibbs adsorption isotherm, Δγ / Δc < 0

  • geometor/etc (Minimal boundary potentials and interfacial equilibria): Spontaneous solute concentration at fluid interfaces supplies the physical precursor to pellicle and spicular deposition.

Plateau’s 120° Triple Junction, Steiner Networks, and Hexagonal Epithelia¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. VII (Forms of Tissues, or Cell-Aggregates) (pp. 293–326)
Summary: Shows that hexagonal cellular pavements, segmentation cleavage planes (Sachs’s & Errera’s laws), and epithelial mosaics arise directly from liquid surface-tension equilibrium.

  • Exact Invariants: ∠APB = ∠BPC = ∠CPA = 120°, s(120°) = 3/4, Euler V - E + F = χ

  • geometor/etc (X(13) (1st Isogonic / Fermat–Torricelli–Steiner Point)): Three soap-film laminae spanning three pins converge physically at X(13), subtending 120° between every pair of segments.

  • geometor/wildberger (Rational Spread s = 3/4): The 120° soap-film equilibrium angle corresponds to exact rational spread sin²(120°) = 3/4 over Q.

  • geometor/pappus (Synagoge Book V, Preface (On the Sagacity of Bees)): Pappus’s proof that the regular hexagon encloses maximum area per unit perimeter among plane-tiling polygons becomes physical capillary minimization in Thompson.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase (1920), Ch. I — hexagonal crystal/honeycomb tiling as the archetype of √3 Static Symmetry

Maraldi’s Tetrahedral Angle (109° 28′ 16″), Rhombic Dodecahedron, and Kelvin’s Tetrakaidecahedron¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. VII (The Bee’s Cell & Parenchyma) (pp. 327–345)
Summary: Unifies Kepler, Maraldi, Réaumur, König, Maclaurin, Boscovich, Plateau, and Kelvin: both the bee’s pyramidal cell cap and three-dimensional soap froth obey the tetrahedral spread s = 8/9.

  • Exact Invariants: cos θ = -1/3, tan(θ/2) = √2, s = 8/9, 14-hedron: F = 14, V = 24, E = 36

  • geometor/wildberger (Tetrahedral Rational Spread s = 8/9): The Maraldi angle arccos(-1/3) ≈ 109° 28′ 16″ has cos²θ = 1/9 and exact rational spread s = 1 - 1/9 = 8/9, with rhombus diagonal quadrance ratio 1 : 2.

  • geometor/pacioli (De Divina Proportione (1509) & Kepler Strena (1611)): Connects semi-regular/space-filling polyhedra—Kepler’s rhombic dodecahedron and Kelvin’s truncated octahedron (8 hexagons + 6 squares)—to physical foam packing.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): The Diagonal (1919–1920), p. 18 — 1 : √2 diagonal ratio of the Maraldi rhombus matches the root-two rectangle

Sachs’s Orthogonal Law, Errera’s Minimal-Area Partitions, and Embryonic Cleavage¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. VIII (The Forms of Tissues, continued) (pp. 346–410)
Summary: Demonstrates that embryonic cell cleavage planes obey physical minimal-surface laws (Errera) and rectangular intersection (Sachs).

  • Exact Invariants: Orthogonal trajectories, Minimal partition area

  • geometor/euclid (Book I, Def. 10 (Right Angles & Perpendicularity)): Sachs’s law of orthogonal partition (90° intersections) governs dividing cellular blastomeres as minimal area partitions.

Sponge Spicules, Haeckel’s Polyhedral Radiolaria, Euler’s Formula, and Cell Geodetics¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. IX (On Concretions, Spicules, and Spicular Skeletons) (pp. 411–487)
Summary: Proves via Euler’s theorem that no spherical radiolarian mesh can consist solely of hexagons, and explains tetrahedral/octahedral/icosahedral baskets via symmetrical soap-film partitions.

  • Exact Invariants: V - E + F = 2, ∑ (6 - k) F_k = 12 (3-valent spherical net)

  • geometor/euclid (Book XIII, Props. 13–18 (Construction & Exhaustion of the Five Regular Solids)): Critiques Haeckel’s claim of true hexagonal/octagonal regular lattices on spheres via Euler’s polyhedral theorem (12 pentagons mandatory in hexagonal fullerene/radiolarian cages, e.g. Aulonia hexagona).

  • geometor/pacioli (De Divina Proportione, Skeletal Polyhedra Plates): Callimitra and Challenger radiolarian frames physically instantiate skeletal wire-frame polyhedra via adsorbed siliceous deposition along Plateau film edges.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase (1920) — icosahedral/dodecahedral √5 proportions in nature

Cylindrical Geodesics, Elastic Bending Energy, and Plant Thickenings¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. X (A Parenthetic Note on Geodetics) (pp. 488–492)
Summary: Formulates plant vessel spiral thickenings and Spirogyra ribbons as shortest paths (geodesics) minimizing elastic bending energy.

  • Exact Invariants: Geodesic curvature κ_g = 0, Helical pitch angle

  • geometor/euclid (Book XI (Geometry of Cylinders and Helices)): Helical bands in xylem vessels and Spirogyra chloroplasts minimize elastic strain energy along cylindrical geodesics.

Spira Mirabilis, Hero’s Theory of the Gnomon, Golden Triangles, and Turbinated Shells¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XI (The Logarithmic Spiral) (pp. 493–586)
Summary: Reveals why Nautilus, ammonites, gastropods, foraminifera, claws, and horns all obey the equiangular spiral: accretion of dead shell or horn at a growing margin without changing shape is mathematically equivalent to gnomonic self-similarity.

  • Exact Invariants: r = a e^(θ cot α), φ² = φ + 1, φ = (1 + √5)/2

  • geometor/euclid (Book II, Def. 2 & Prop. 11; Book IV, Prop. 10; Book VI, Prop. 30): Generalizes Euclid’s parallelogrammic gnomon (II Def. 2) via Hero Def. 58 to the whirling-square golden rectangle (II.11, VI.30) and the 36°–72°–72° golden triangle (IV.10).

  • geometor/aristotle (Categories 15a29–33): Recovers Aristotle’s law that gnomonic accretion increases magnitude without altering form (αὐξάνεται μὲν, ἀλλοιοῦται δ’ οὔ).

  • geometor/divine (Golden Ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2 & Gnomonic Chains): Thompson’s Figs. 252 and 254 provide the classical morphological realization of recursive φ gnomons.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): The Diagonal (1919–1920), Lesson I & Whirling Squares — direct citation of logarithmic shell growth and square gnomons of the φ rectangle

Pulsatile Chamber Accretion and Geometric Progressions in Foraminifera¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XII (The Spiral Shells of the Foraminifera) (pp. 587–611)
Summary: Shows how discrete fluid droplet extrusions generate linear, alternating, and equiangular chambered foraminiferal tests.

  • Exact Invariants: Discrete gnomonic ratio, Geometric volume ratio

  • geometor/euclid (Book IX, Prop. 35 (Sum of Geometric Progression)): Pulsatile extrusion of successive chambers in Nodosaria, Textularia, and Rotalia forms discrete geometric sequences.

Differential Accretion, Conical Envelopes, and 3D Spatial Torsion¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XIII (The Shapes of Horns, and of Teeth or Tusks) (pp. 612–634)
Summary: Extends gnomonic accretion to 3D horns, teeth, and tusks where unequal growth rates across the generative margin induce curvature and torsion.

  • Exact Invariants: Spatial torsion τ, Differential marginal growth

  • geometor/euclid (Book XII (Pyramids, Cones, and Space Curves)): Moseley’s equations of horn curves combine transverse taper with longitudinal curvature and spatial torsion.

Schimper–Braun Fractions, Cylindrical Parastichy Lattices, and Golden Divergence¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XIV (On Leaf-Arrangement, or Phyllotaxis) (pp. 635–651)
Summary: Demystifies botanical Fibonacci numbers through Bravais’s cylindrical lattice, P. G. Tait’s overlapping parastichy theorem, A. H. Church’s equipotential spirals, and Hofmeister’s mechanical apex rule.

  • Exact Invariants: Δθ / 2π = 1 / φ² = (3 - √5)/2 ≈ 0.381966, F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}

  • geometor/phyllotaxis (Golden Angle 2π/φ², Cylindrical & Vogel Planar Parastichies): Provides the verbatim 1917 historical foundation for geometor/phyllotaxis: continued-fraction convergents [0; 2, 1, 1, 1, …] → φ⁻² and Tait’s (m, n) → m + n lattice geometry.

  • geometor/divine (Continued Fraction of φ & Fibonacci Convergents): Explains why successive Fibonacci ratios F_k / F_{k+2} emerge automatically from mechanical close-packing without mystical teleology.

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): The Diagonal (1919–1920), Vol. I, No. 1, pp. 1–7, 21 — A. H. Church’s sunflower cross-curves (34/55, 55/89) as the biological fountainhead of Dynamic Symmetry

Oviduct Peristalsis, Axisymmetric Membrane Statics, and Bird Egg Geometries¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XV (On the Shapes of Eggs, and of Certain Other Hollow Structures) (pp. 652–669)
Summary: Derives bird-egg shapes from peristaltic muscle pressures acting on pliable membranes, refuting cliff-ledge teleology.

  • Exact Invariants: T_n / R_n + T_t / R_t = P_n, Peristaltic pressure gradient

  • geometor/pappus (Synagoge Book V (Equilibrium of Fluids and Elastic Membranes)): Laplace’s membrane equilibrium equation governs the conical and pyriform shapes assumed by flexible eggshells under hydrostatic pressure.

Peristaltic Egg Mechanics, Culmann’s Crane, Meyer’s Femur Trabeculae, and Fidler’s Cantilever Backbones¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XVI (On Form and Mechanical Efficiency) (pp. 670–718)
Summary: Shows first how oviduct peristalsis shapes conical and elliptical bird eggs, and second how cancellous bone trabeculae and quadruped/dinosaur spines embody exact graphic-statics stress diagrams.

  • Exact Invariants: T_n / R_n + T_t / R_t = P (avian egg membrane), σ₁ ⊥ σ₂ (orthogonal principal stresses)

  • geometor/pappus (Projective & Reciprocal Graphical Constructions): Culmann’s graphic statics and Maxwell–Cremona–Fidler reciprocal truss diagrams apply projective polarity directly to skeletal stress lines.

Dürer’s Grids, Cartesian Deformation Meshes, and Comparative Phylogeny¶

Chapter & Locus: Ch. XVII (On the Theory of Transformations, or the Comparison of Related Forms) (pp. 719–779)
Summary: Culminates On Growth and Form: related species differ not by piecemeal mosaic mutations of isolated parts, but by single global coordinate transformations of the organism as an integral whole.

  • Exact Invariants: (x, y) ↦ (x + y cot ω, y sin ω), Conformal & coaxial circle families

  • geometor/pappus (Synagoge Book VII (Projective Cross-Ratio & Collineations)): Thompson’s affine shear, perspective/radial, and coaxial circle transformations preserve harmonic and projective invariants across related organic genera.

  • geometor/hilbert (Grundlagen der Geometrie, Affine & Projective Transformation Groups): Realizes Felix Klein’s and Hilbert’s Erlangen transformation-group viewpoint in comparative anatomy (Argyropelecus → Sternoptyx, Diodon → Orthagoriscus, Eohippus → Equus).

  • Jay Hambidge (1919–1920): The Diagonal (1919–1920) — Dürer’s human proportion grids contrasted with classical Greek areal modulation
