Chapter XI: The Logarithmic Spiral — Summary¶

Analytical Synopsis — 1917 Cambridge First Edition — Chapter XI (pp. 493–586)

Overview of Chapter XI (The Logarithmic Spiral)¶

Chapter XI presents the geometric centerpiece of On Growth and Form—the theory of the equiangular spiral and gnomonic self-similarity:

  1. The Logarithmic Spiral (pp. 493–507): Contrasts the Archimedean spiral ($r = a\theta$, archimedes:spirals) with the equiangular spiral of Descartes and Jakob Bernoulli ($r = a e^{\theta \cot\alpha}$, bernoulli:1692).

  2. Hero’s Theory of the Gnomon (pp. 508–514): Recovers Aristotle's (Categories 15a30, aristotle:cat) and Hero of Alexandria's (Definitiones 58, hero:def) doctrine of the gnomon: terminal accretion preserving shape requires gnomonic growth.

  3. Whirling Squares & Golden Triangles (pp. 515–530): Illustrates gnomons on the $\sqrt{2}$ rectangle, the whirling-square golden rectangle ($1 : \phi$), and Euclid's IV.10 golden triangle.

  4. Helicospiral Shells (pp. 531–586): Develops Canon Henry Moseley's 3D helicospiral parameters $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ for turbinated gastropods, bivalves, and Nautilus (moseley:1838).
